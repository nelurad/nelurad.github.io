I study how minds turn written words, speech, and gesture into meaning, and whether the artificial systems we build to model language arrive there the same way. My work approaches this from two complementary directions:

  1. Examining human cognition: how we read, produce, and interpret language, and how the brain supports the seemingly effortless ways we communicate.
  2. Building and evaluating artificial systems: my earlier work developed neural networks for sign language recognition and emoji synthesis. More recently, I've turned to the question of how biological and artificial intelligence should be compared, and what computational models can genuinely tell us about the mind.

My years wrangling both text and toddlers continue to shape how I think about naturalistic cognition, and much of my research is carried out alongside undergraduate and high school researchers.

I am currently pursuing my doctorate at Florida State University, and previously completed postgraduate studies at both the University of Colorado Boulder and the University at Buffalo after earning my undergraduate degree from the University of Texas at Austin.
